KHIPUAI, North America

Three ways to win a US or Canadian buyer without the rope.

Wil's point: a visitor from Peru reads the khipu as heritage, while a visitor from Toronto or Ohio sees knotted string and has to work out why it is there. The current site stays as it is for Peru and Latin America. These are three directions for the version shown to North American visitors.

All three keep the KHIPUAI name, the small knot logo, the same offer, the same prices, and the same wording from shared/content.js. Only the look and the story change, so the three can be compared fairly.

A. The Map

Show their own back office

Light, clean, software-company feel. The hero is a small diagram of one invoice moving through an inbox, a PDF, a spreadsheet, an approval and the accounting system. A switch flips it from "Today" (people retyping and chasing) to "Automated" (a person still approves).

Says
We understand how your operation actually runs.
Best for
Ops and finance leaders who think in workflows.
Effort
Medium. New light theme alongside the dark one.

B. The Count

Let them count the hours

The current site exactly as it is (dark navy and copper, one screen per section, scrolling sideways), with the khipu taken out. The opening photograph is replaced by a 10-second calculator: people, hours a week, cost per hour, giving hours and dollars lost a year. The line across the site keeps its glow but loses its knots. Every number is the visitor's own input.

Says
This is costing you real money right now.
Best for
Owners and CFOs who decide on numbers.
Effort
Lowest. Same page and code as today, a new opening screen.

How the region switch would work

Showing a different site by country, on the setup we have today.

The site runs on GitHub Pages: plain files, no server. A plain file host cannot see a visitor's IP address, so Wil's idea needs one of these:

Option 1: Cloudflare in front of the site (real IP-based)

  • Cloudflare's free plan sits between visitors and GitHub Pages and knows each visitor's country.
  • A small rule (a Cloudflare Worker) sends Peru and Latin America to the current site and the US and Canada to the North America version.
  • Needs a DNS change on khipuai.co, which is Wil's decision. No cost on the free tier for this traffic.

Option 2: decide in the browser (no new service)

  • The page reads the visitor's time zone and language (for example America/Lima or America/Toronto) and picks a version.
  • Free and private. Wrong for some travellers and VPN users, so it is a best guess, not a lookup.

Either way

  • Give each version its own address (for example khipuai.co for North America and khipuai.co/pe/ for Peru) and only redirect people the first time. Showing different pages at the same address can hurt Google ranking, and Google crawls mostly from the US, so it would mostly see the North America version.
  • Always show a visible switch in the header ("Peru / North America"), so anyone placed in the wrong version can move.
  • The existing Spanish site at /es/ is the natural home for the Peru version.
Decisions for Wil

Before any of this is built for real

  • Which direction, or which parts to combine. A likely mix: B's look and calculator, with A's workflow diagram lower down, and C's "Why the name" footer.
  • Option 1 or Option 2 for the switch, and which countries go to which version.
  • Whether the knot logo stays for North America. All three concepts keep it.
  • Wording of the "Why the name" story in C, which is a new historical statement on the site.
  • Concept B's example cost per hour ($45) is only a starting slider position; confirm it, or start the slider blank.
